## 1. Executive Summary

This audit assesses the methodology, mathematical foundations, and interpretation risks of the feature importance pipeline in `IncomeLens-AI`.

### Key Conclusions:
1. **Importance Methodology:** The script extracts **Mean Decrease in Impurity (MDI / Gini Importance)** directly from the fitted `RandomForestClassifier` (`classifier.feature_importances_`). It is **neither permutation-based nor SHAP-based**.
2. **Data Utilization Discrepancy:** The previous implementation loaded `train.csv` and `validation.csv` and computed `X_transformed`, but **never used this data**; it simply read `classifier.feature_importances_`. MDI is an in-sample metric computed during training tree construction, not an out-of-sample validation metric. The script has been cleaned up and explicitly annotated to prevent misinterpretation.
3. **Cardinality & Continuous Feature Selection Bias:** Continuous features with high distinct values (e.g., `fnlwgt`, sampling weight) are favored by Gini splits and ranked artificially high (#7 with 6.23% importance), despite lacking economic predictive power.
4. **Collinearity Distortion:** Highly correlated features (e.g., `marital-status_Married-civ-spouse` vs. `relationship_Husband`, or collinear one-hot pairs `sex_Female` and `sex_Male`) split split-counts across trees, diluting individual importance.
5. **Non-Causal Interpretation:** MDI reflects internal model mechanics (which features were split upon), not real-world causal mechanisms. The updated plot and script now clearly label the metric as `Mean Decrease in Impurity (Gini Importance)` with an explicit non-causal disclaimer.
6. **Artifact Integrity & Tests:** The CSV output structure and figure have been validated and covered by automated regression tests in [`tests/test_feature_importance.py`](file:///d:/incomelens-ai/tests/test_feature_importance.py).

## 2. Core Methodology: Mean Decrease in Impurity (MDI)

### 2.1 Mathematical Formulation
For each decision tree $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ in the random forest ensemble ($T=150$):
- Let $N$ be an internal node split by feature $j$.
- Let $p(N)$ be the proportion of training samples reaching node $N$.
- The Gini impurity reduction $\Delta i(N)$ at node $N$ is:
  $$\Delta i(N) = I(N) - \left( \frac{N_{\text{left}}}{N} I(N_{\text{left}}) + \frac{N_{\text{right}}}{N} I(N_{\text{right}}) \right)$$
- The importance of feature $j$ in tree $t$ is the sum of impurity reductions across all nodes split by $j$:
  $$\text{Imp}_t(j) = \sum_{N \in t : \text{split}(N) = j} p(N) \Delta i(N)$$
- The forest MDI is the average over all trees, normalized to sum to 1:
  $$\text{MDI}(j) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \text{Imp}_t(j)$$

### 2.2 Output Representation in `IncomeLens-AI`
- **Total Features:** 105 transformed features generated by `ColumnTransformer` (StandardScaler on 6 numeric features, OneHotEncoder on 8 categorical features).
- **Importances Sum:** $\sum_{j=1}^{105} \text{MDI}(j) = 1.000000$.
- **Zero-Importance Features:** 1 feature (`categorical__native-country_Holand-Netherlands` = 0.0) was never selected for a split due to having only 1 record in the dataset.

## 4. Methodological Limitations & Empirical Comparison

### 4.1 Cardinality & Continuous Feature Bias (`fnlwgt`)
A classical limitation of MDI is selection bias toward continuous features and categorical features with many unique levels:
- `numeric__fnlwgt` (Census demographic expansion weight) has tens of thousands of unique numeric values.
- In MDI, `numeric__fnlwgt` is ranked **#7** with **6.23%** of total impurity reduction.
- However, sampling weight (`fnlwgt`) has negligible causal or behavioral relationship to personal earnings. It is chosen frequently in deep trees simply because its high number of candidate split thresholds offers opportunistic empirical impurity reductions on training partitions.

### 4.2 Collinearity & Feature Dilution
When two or more features share high mutual information:
1. **Marital Status vs. Relationship:**
   - `categorical__marital-status_Married-civ-spouse`: 10.10% (Rank 2)
   - `categorical__relationship_Husband`: 8.12% (Rank 5)
   - `categorical__relationship_Wife`: 1.54% (Rank 12)
   These features represent nearly identical demographic facts. In tree splits, trees alternate between splitting on `Married-civ-spouse` and `Husband`, diluting the individual importance of each.
2. **Education Level vs. Years:**
   - `numeric__education-num`: 8.93% (Rank 4)
   - `categorical__education_Bachelors`: 2.06% (Rank 11)
   - `categorical__education_Masters`: 1.36% (Rank 14)
   - `categorical__education_Prof-school`: 0.95% (Rank 18)
   - `categorical__education_HS-grad`: 0.88% (Rank 20)
   `education-num` captures the linear progression of schooling, while one-hot categories capture discrete credential milestones. Both compete for splits.
3. **Collinear One-Hot Pairs:**
   - `categorical__sex_Female`: 1.34% (Rank 16)
   - `categorical__sex_Male`: 1.21% (Rank 17)
   Because `drop='first'` was not used during one-hot encoding, binary sex is split into two perfectly inverse features, diluting the attribute across two columns.

### 4.3 Empirical Comparison: MDI vs. Permutation vs. SHAP

## 5. Non-Causal Reporting & Presentation Guidelines

### 5.1 Why MDI is Non-Causal
1. **No Counterfactual Validity:** High MDI indicates that a feature is frequently used to split partitions in the training trees. It does not mean that changing an individual's marital status or hours worked will causally alter their income.
2. **Directional Ambiguity:** MDI is strictly non-negative ($\ge 0$). It does not convey whether higher feature values increase or decrease income probability (unlike SHAP).
3. **Confounding:** Spurious correlations and demographic proxies (e.g., marital status proxying age, career tenure, dual-earner households) are captured by trees without confounding adjustment.

### 5.2 Reporting Recommendations
- Always label the chart and metric as **"Mean Decrease in Impurity (Gini Importance)"**, never as "Feature Influence" or "Impact".
- Include an explicit note: *"Impurity-based importance reflects training split frequency and is non-causal."*
- For individual or directional explanations, refer stakeholders to [`reports/shap_validation.md`](file:///d:/incomelens-ai/reports/shap_validation.md) and [`reports/figures/shap_summary.png`](file:///d:/incomelens-ai/reports/figures/shap_summary.png).
- For out-of-sample generalization importance, report permutation importance on unseen validation data.
